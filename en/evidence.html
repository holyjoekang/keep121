We ran each of 10 teams' itineraries 241 times under four conditions — manual, Keep Tag, Keep Tag+ and Shelter — and added 7 sensitivity analyses. Most of the effect came not from automating attendance but from knowing where people who drifted away during free time were.

How to read the chart

Each row is one team's trip. Gray dots are Keep Tag, purple dots with a number are Keep Tag+, and red-ringed dots are Shelter; the further right, the more risk was reduced. The 5 teams in the shaded area at the top have itineraries that include someone getting separated during free time.

What does the % mean?

It is how much risk exposure fell, taking manual operation (the guide calling names) as 100. Formula: (manual − condition) ÷ manual × 100. E.g. T06 Gyeongju school trip: manual 396 → Keep Tag+ 167, so (396−167) ÷ 396 ≈ 58% less. 0% means the same as manual, 100% means all risk removed, and a negative value means worse than manual. Each value is the average of 241 runs of the same itinerary under random conditions.

Why didn't Keep Tag help?

Keep Tag (Bluetooth only) is visible only near the guide's phone. When someone wanders far away during free time, nobody knows where they are, so the difference from manual stayed within ±8%. Keep Tag+ (BLE + GPS + cellular) reports the location even when far away, cutting risk exposure by 39–58% in the same teams. Shelter is a mode that switches Keep Tag+ to continuous tracking only on risky stretches.

Keep121 simulator · 10 teams × 4 conditions × 241 seeds, run on 2026-10-16. All figures come from assumed values and will be calibrated with field samples.

Six decisions the simulation changed

01Keep Tag+ is the default for itineraries with free time

In free-time incident teams, Keep Tag's risk exposure differs from manual by only ±4%, because no one knows where people beyond the guide's range are.

02Shelter is a "stretch mode," not a device

Tracking all the time drains a hiking team's tag by 307 mAh a day — empty in 1.2 days. It turns on only during stretches marked as risky.

03Alert design is the product

A 120-student team gets about 25 false alarms a day; cut the hub-exit wait to 60 seconds and it's 100. Three levels + bundling + auto-suppression.

04The guide's phone is the key device

If the hub's scan success rate drops from 0.85 to 0.3, false alarms rise from 24 to 51. We recommend an Android hub with a keep-screen-on mode.

05One-touch roll call is standard on every product

A single check went from 112–1,248 seconds of calling names to 18–55 seconds with one touch.

06For families, "confirmed" beats coordinates

Location updates within 1 minute on average are already enough. The default screen shows spot-level location plus the time the guide confirmed.
